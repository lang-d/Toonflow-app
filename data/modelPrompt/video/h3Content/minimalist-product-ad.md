# H3 single-shot expression skill: minimalist product advertisement

## Scope and fact inheritance

Use this layer only to compile a supplied Toonflow shot as a restrained product-advertising shot. It is not a brief-writing, copywriting, product-analysis, anchor-image, multi-beat commercial, music, or editing workflow. A visible product, its variation, material, colour, mechanism, copy, logo, or benefit exists only when the current storyboard facts or an explicitly defined reference role establishes it. Do not promote an ordinary prop, a character asset, or an unlabelled reference picture into a product hero.

The formal `videoStyle` is the authority for medium, lighting, palette, realism, and rendering. Preserve the exact product identity, silhouette, colour, material finish, markings, and visible state supplied by the facts and references. This layer may make a confirmed product action more readable; it may not recolour, redesign, beautify, replace, or add a product feature.

## Product-shot visual grammar

Choose one evidenced visual lead for the shot: a hero silhouette, a material/structure detail, a visible mechanism, a confirmed variant relation, or supplied in-frame copy. Do not make two independent leads compete. Keep the product silhouette and the action-result legible before adding camera emphasis. If the facts establish a calm or uncluttered composition, preserve usable negative space rather than filling it with decorative technology, particles, UI, mirrors, grids, panels, or product-wall layouts.

Drive motion from an actual edge, highlight, opening, rotation, slide, snap, fold, screen change, contact, or other explicitly described product state. A camera push, pull, orbit, pan, tilt, or focus shift may clarify that real change, but never substitute for it. Do not create white flashes, random light sweeps, fake metrics, abstract particles, or an arbitrary cut merely to make the result feel premium.

When multiple confirmed variants are in the same shot, establish the stated main variant first; introduce supporting variants only at the fact-supported moment and without covering the main silhouette. Do not turn variants into a matrix, split-screen, catalogue wall, or unexplained colour parade.

## Timeline compilation

Write the H3 timeline as one observable progression: confirmed opening product state -> one primary visible action or reveal -> confirmed settled result. For a short shot, use one readable change and a stable finish; for a longer supplied shot, allow a preparation, impact, and brief settle only when all states are evidenced. Secondary changes must trail the primary action rather than occur simultaneously. Preserve supplied camera movement and use any additional wording only to make that movement's existing purpose explicit.

If exact on-screen copy is supplied, reproduce it verbatim in its stated language and time. Treat it as an integrated visual element, not a subtitle: one confirmed line at a time, placed only where the facts or reference composition supports it. Describe its entry or hold only when the source specifies it. Never invent a slogan, translation, font name, logo, CTA, claim, feature label, or text colour. If no copy is supplied, write no copy instructions.

## Missing-fact policy and boundaries

Omit a product claim, user interaction, hand, variant, typography, logo, camera move, or sound event when it is not evidenced. Do not infer product function from its name, category, or appearance. Do not add BGM, score, launch music, narration, audience reaction, or advertising voice-over. Existing dialogue and diegetic sound stay verbatim and in chronological position.

Do not override storyboard order or duration, keyframe/reference roles, `<Picture N>` / `<Video N>` / `<Audio N>` mapping, dialogue, visible text, diegetic sound, or the no-BGM rule. This layer never replaces the formal `videoStyle`.

## Final check

Before output, confirm that the prompt identifies one fact-supported visual lead, preserves product identity, makes the stated action-result readable, and contains no invented copy, benefit, logo, hand, setting, or music.
