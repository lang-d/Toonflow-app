# H3 single-shot expression skill: 3D animation short

## Scope and fact inheritance

Use this layer to turn an already planned Toonflow shot into a physically readable animated performance. It is not the official end-to-end 3D-short workflow: do not create a project brief, story outline, character card, scene card, new storyboard, shot-table hook, pencil panel, assembly plan, or BGM. The formal `videoStyle` remains authoritative; do not impose Pixar, C4D, cartoon proportions, squash-and-stretch, or any other house style unless those qualities are already established by `videoStyle` or the current shot facts.

Keep every confirmed character, prop, landmark, screen direction, starting pose, action result, and camera instruction intact. Do not turn `voiceTone` into facial acting, and do not add a character, reaction, gag, chase, cut, or dialogue to make the shot more animated.

## Animation staging grammar

Describe a clear line of action and a readable pose hierarchy: the primary subject and action must be intelligible from silhouette, weight direction, contact point, and prop relationship. When the supplied action includes contact, lifting, stopping, turning, entering, exiting, or carrying, preserve the support point, weight transfer, and final balance. When a change is explicitly rapid or expressive, make the already evidenced preparation, action, overshoot, follow-through, or settle legible; otherwise do not manufacture elastic performance.

Use the established space as an anchor. Preserve stated left/right orientation, eyeline, foreground/background relation, prop location, and any confirmed lighting baseline. A camera move can follow the primary action, reveal its result, or maintain an existing spatial relationship. Do not add an orbit, Dutch angle, close-up, or reframing merely because animated shorts often use one.

## Timeline compilation

Organize each supplied shot into continuous animated beats: starting pose and spatial anchor -> necessary preparation or trigger -> primary action with readable contact/weight -> confirmed end pose or result. The timeline must cover the supplied duration without adding a new shot. Make each movement lead naturally into the next stated state; do not write vague filler such as “continues moving.” If the facts contain a small action only, retain the small action rather than expanding it into an exaggerated sequence.

Place dialogue and diegetic sound at their supplied moments. Audio may support a confirmed contact, movement, or pause, but it must not invent exertion noises, comedy effects, narration, or music. Preserve the base H3 keyframe and reference semantics exactly.

## Missing-fact policy and boundaries

When a pose, expression, landmark, lighting change, secondary motion, or camera move is absent, omit it. Do not infer a visual expression from `voiceTone`, a character trait from an asset name, or a hook from genre. Do not add BGM, score, soundtrack, or audience-only music.

Do not override the formal `videoStyle`, storyboard chronology and duration, keyframe/reference roles, media labels, dialogue, visible text, diegetic sound, or the no-BGM rule.

## Final check

The final prompt must make the supplied action physically traceable from opening pose to ending pose, keep spatial anchors stable, and contain no genre-default animation, new performance, or unsupported camera cut.
