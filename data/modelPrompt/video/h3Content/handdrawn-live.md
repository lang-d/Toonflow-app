# H3 single-shot expression skill: hand-drawn and live-action fusion

## Scope and fact inheritance

Use this layer only when the formal `videoStyle` and current storyboard facts explicitly establish live-action space fused with a hand-drawn animated entity or a confirmed hand-drawn-to-live transformation. It adapts one existing shot; it does not invent a 15-second concept, new room, contact object, hand, creature, transformation route, chase, joke, ending, or live-action treatment.

Keep the supplied subject, space, physical contact, motion direction, camera, transformation result, and tone exactly intact. Do not force rough glowing drawing, crayon, chalk, coloured-pencil, pastel, phone-camera, or hand-held aesthetics when the existing `videoStyle` and facts do not establish them.

## Fusion and contact grammar

When supported, make the boundary between live-action matter and drawn matter physically traceable. State the confirmed contact point—such as a supplied hand, object, surface, edge, or opening—then retain that relationship as the same drawn entity changes form or moves. Preserve residue or a visible continuity cue only when the storyboard describes it. A transformation must be continuous: the next form grows from, folds out of, or carries a visible aspect of the prior confirmed form; never swap in an unrelated new character.

Use the existing space as a continuous route. Camera movement may start from the supplied framing and follow the fact-supported subject slightly after it moves, so the pursuit feels observed rather than pre-aimed. Do not create a cut to another location, an unexplained jump scare, a hostile creature, huge eyes, teeth, darkness, or horror language.

## Timeline compilation

Map the supplied duration proportionally rather than forcing the official 15-second intervals: opening establishes the confirmed contact/space -> middle carries the stated continuous morph or movement -> ending reaches the supplied action result or spatial change. Each phase needs an evidenced change; do not pad the shot with invented tricks. If the official source's delayed camera-follow grammar applies to the facts, express it through the supplied camera movement without overriding a locked camera instruction.

Keep existing dialogue and diegetic sound in their stated place. Describe pencil/chalk friction, small rustle, scrape, tap, room tone, or movement sound only when supported by the visible material/action or supplied sound facts. Never add BGM, score, narrator, scream, or audience reaction.

## Missing-fact policy and boundaries

Without an explicit live/drawn fusion, contact, continuous transformation, or chase path, omit that construction entirely. Do not infer it from an asset name, a voice tone, or a type selection. Do not add a human hand, new prop, new setting, transformation, caption, music, or visual horror.

Do not override the formal `videoStyle`, storyboard chronology and duration, keyframe/reference roles, media labels, dialogue, visible text, diegetic sound, or the no-BGM rule.

## Final check

The final prompt must make every supported fusion/contact/transformation continuous in one factual space and preserve the supplied end state, without generating a new creative premise.
