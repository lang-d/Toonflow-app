# H3 single-shot expression skill: papercraft stop-motion explainer

## Scope and fact inheritance

Use this layer only when the formal `videoStyle` or current storyboard facts already establish a papercraft, cut-paper, cardboard, pop-up-book, miniature-diorama, or stop-motion treatment. It adapts one existing shot; it does not choose an educational topic, create a visual metaphor, redesign characters or sets, create an asset library, rewrite narration, sequence a full explainer, or assemble a film.

Preserve the supplied subject, factual action, location, composition, camera movement, dialogue, and sound. Paper material is an expression of an already established treatment, not permission to convert a realistic, 3D, hand-drawn, or other formal `videoStyle` into paper craft.

## Papercraft space and material grammar

When supported, describe readable physical construction: layered cardstock or cardboard planes, cut edges, paper fibres, folds, seams, tabs, hinges, slots, brads, pull-tabs, sliders, rotating discs, page turns, or visible thickness. Retain only materials and mechanisms compatible with the existing subject and facts; do not add labels, diagrams, tools, hands, or instructional props.

Build depth from the established composition: a readable focal action in the middle plane, foreground occlusion only when it exists or is implied by the supplied paper scene, and background planes that separate through the stated camera or scene motion. Do not flatten the shot into vector graphics, make it glossy plastic 3D, or introduce unexplained new scenery merely to create parallax.

## Stop-motion timeline compilation

Express the confirmed action through discrete but coherent handmade movement: a small stepped move, a purposeful pause, a hinge/slide/pull action, a slight paper rebound, then the confirmed settled state. Keep the movement frame-by-frame in feel without describing technical frame rates. Environmental movement may use an existing paper mechanism—such as a stated door, cloud, leaf, disc, or backdrop—only if the storyboard supports that object and change.

Use camera movement to reveal established layers or follow the supplied focal action; avoid smooth digital drift, empty zooms, or unrelated cuts. Preserve existing dialogue. When paper sound is already supported, make it tactile and tied to the visible action—soft rustle, slide, tap, fold, pop, or settle—rather than adding a generic soundtrack.

## Missing-fact policy and boundaries

If paper material, a layered set, a mechanism, a paper sound, or a transformation is not supported, omit it. Never invent narrator speech, educational labels, captions, diagrams, hands, a new knowledge step, BGM, or music. Do not change a factual action into a demonstration.

Do not override the formal `videoStyle`, shot chronology and duration, keyframe/reference roles, media labels, dialogue, visible text, diegetic sound, or the no-BGM rule.

## Final check

The prompt must retain the current shot while making any supported paper construction, depth, stepped movement, and tactile sound visibly consequential—not merely attaching the word “papercraft” to a generic animation.
